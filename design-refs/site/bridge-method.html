What's broken in adult ESOL

Most platforms throw English at learners who don't understand the instructions.

01 · English first apps

Demand the very skill they are meant to teach.

Adults stall on instructions, quietly drop out at A1, never come back. The first month is the entire experiment.

02 · Translation overlays

Word for word swaps break grammar.

Learners memorise sentences they cannot recombine. Move one word and the meaning collapses. Real conversation fails.

03 · Generic AI tutors

No ESOL pedagogy, no UK context, no evidence.

Useful for hobbyists. Not built for funded provision. No RARPA stages, no ILR export, no safeguarding overlay.

The six layers

One conversation. Six concurrent pedagogical jobs.

The six layers stack into a single Gemini AI prompt. The learner experiences one normal conversation. Under the hood, six teaching jobs run on every turn.

01 · L1 anchor

Anchored in the learner's first language.

Every new word and structure is introduced in the learner's first language so meaning is never in doubt. The instructions never become the obstacle.

02 · Bridge utterance

The bridge between L1 and English.

The AI issues the same idea in an L1 + English hybrid ("I have a cita with the docter") — recognised, then gently corrected.

03 · Independent English

Unaided attempt, live scored.

The learner attempts the utterance in English alone. The AI scores against a five band rubric in real time. Strength and slip both surfaced.

04 · UK context anchor

Real adult UK scenarios, never generic.

GP receptionist, Universal Credit appointment, school parents' evening. Not "the cat is on the mat." Same day useful.

05 · Safeguarding overlay

Pre cached keyword and intent detection.

Domestic violence, mental health and child welfare disclosures route to the DSL inside five seconds. Every turn screened.

06 · Evidence capture

RARPA grade evidence, every turn.

Each turn writes RARPA grade evidence to the learner's ledger automatically. Stages 1 to 5 reports build themselves.
