1. Signing in

What it's for: getting into your Amber account.

How to get here: open the Amber website and choose Sign in, or go straight to /login.

Step by step

  1. Type your email address.
  2. Type your password.
  3. Press the sign-in button.

You will land on your dashboard. If you have not joined yet, you need an invitation link from your organisation — see the next section.

If something goes wrong

  • Wrong password — check it carefully; ask your teacher or organisation if you need it reset.
  • "Account not enrolled" — your organisation has not set you up for ESOL yet. Contact them.

2. Joining Amber (first time only)

Your organisation sends you a special invitation link. Opening it starts a short, friendly form — about 5 minutes, in five steps (you can see them across the top):

  1. Welcome — what Amber is, in simple words. Note the language picker at the top right — you can read the whole form in your own language. Press Start.

  2. Your details — your name, date of birth, nationality, and your first language. Choose the language you know best — Amber will use it to help you learn. You can choose English, العربية (Arabic), 粵語 (Cantonese), or Türkçe (Turkish).

  3. Eligibility — one declaration to confirm.

  4. ULN — your Unique Learner Number if you know it. You can skip this and add it later.

  5. Done — you're in!

Good to know: if you choose Arabic, the whole app flips to right-to-left reading. You can change language later at any time. Nothing saves until you press Continue, and you can press Back at any step.

Your placement assessment

The first time you sign in, Amber asks you 20 short questions to find your level (from Entry 1 to Level 2). It is not a test you can fail — it just helps Amber pick the right conversations for you.

Read each question and press the answer that feels right. The orange bar at the top shows how far you are. At the end you'll see your level and your first learning goals. Your teacher reviews every placement, so it can be adjusted if it feels wrong.

3. Your dashboard

What it's for: your home page — see your level, jump into practice, and catch anything new.

How to get here: you land here after signing in. Sidebar → Dashboard.

If your teacher has written to you

  • Read it, then press Got it — close.
  • Press Open inbox to see all your messages instead.
  • Press Read later to close it for now — it stays in Messages.

The dashboard itself

4. Practising with the AI Tutor

This is the heart of Amber. A practice session has three parts: Prepare (get ready) → Roleplay (the conversation) → Complete (see how you did).

4.1 Choose a scenario

How to get here: Sidebar → AI Tutor.

  • Your journey shows your scenarios as a path, ending at your next milestone (for example B1-ready · about 14 weeks). Work through them in order — or pick any one you like.
  • Pick a scenario below shows the same conversations as cards, all matched to your level.
  • Each card shows the topic and about how many minutes it takes.

Press any scenario to continue.

4.2 Prepare

What it's for: see what you will practise before you start — no surprises.

You see the situation, the UK context, roughly how long it takes, and the four steps of the conversation (for example: greet the receptionist → describe your symptoms → agree a time → confirm your details).

Press Begin roleplay when you're ready.

4.3 The conversation

What's on this screen:

  • Top: the scenario name and a timer.
  • The four dots — your progress through the conversation's four steps. They fill up as you complete each step ("Step 1 of 4").
  • The chat — Amber speaks first. Type your reply in the box and press Enter (or the send button).
  • T button — makes the text bigger (three sizes).
  • ✕ button — leave the session (your progress is saved — see 4.5).

Step by step

  1. Read Amber's message.
  2. Type your answer in English — the best English you can. Mistakes are welcome; that's how you learn.
  3. Press Enter. Amber replies in a few seconds.
  4. Keep going — the dots fill as the conversation moves forward.

Good to know

  • If you don't understand, say so — write "I don't understand" in English or in your own language. Amber will slow down and use more of your language. This is never a problem.
  • If your provider has voice switched on, you'll also see a Listen button under Amber's messages (hear it aloud) and a microphone button (speak instead of typing). Typing always works too.
  • After a while, a gentle banner may suggest wrapping up. You can dismiss it and keep going — it never stops you.

4.4 Finishing — your results

End a session in either way:

  • finish the conversation naturally, or
  • press End & review session → under the message box.

You see:

  • Your score for the session.
  • Words you have learned.
  • Your tutor's note — what you did well and what to practise next.
  • The emoji row — tell us how the session felt (one tap).

Press Finish to go back to your dashboard. You'll hear a soft chime — well done!

4.5 Leaving early and coming back

Press ✕ during a session and you'll see this:

  • Leave for now — closes the session and saves everything. You can come back any time.
  • End & review — finishes the session now and shows your score.
  • Keep practising — changes nothing; back to the chat.

To come back later: Sidebar → My Sessions → press the session marked In progress. The whole conversation is restored:

If you write about something upsetting

If you tell Amber about something serious — feeling unsafe, being hurt, or feeling very low — the practice pauses and Amber shows you a calm message in your language with real places that can help. A trusted person at your organisation is told, so a human can support you. You are never in trouble for saying how you feel.

5. My Goals

What it's for: the learning goals set after your placement — check they feel right, and agree them.

How to get here: Sidebar → My Goals.

Step by step

  1. Read the goals — they're written for your level.
  2. (Optional) add anything you'd like in the box — for example, "I also want to practise phone calls."
  3. Press These goals look right.

Good to know: agreeing your goals is recorded — it's part of your official learning record. Want to change them later? Speak to your teacher any time.

6. Vocabulary

What it's for: every word you meet in sessions is saved here so you can revisit it.

How to get here: Sidebar → Vocabulary.

What you see:

  • Total words · Mastered · To practise at the top.
  • One card per word: which scenario it came from, when, and how many times you've seen it.
  • I know this / Need practice — tap to tell Amber. Words you keep using well become "learned" automatically too.
  • Search and level filter at the top.

Amber quietly brings your "to practise" words back into future conversations — that's how they stick.

7. My Sessions

What it's for: every session you've ever started, newest first.

How to get here: Sidebar → My Sessions.

  • Done — finished sessions. Press one to re-read the whole conversation (you can't type in a finished session).
  • In progress — press to continue where you left off.

8. Messages

What it's for: notes from your teacher, in your language.

How to get here: Sidebar → Messages.

New messages also appear as a banner on your dashboard — and Amber makes sure you see them before your next session. When there's nothing new, you'll see "You're all caught up."

11. If something goes wrong

Problem What to do
Amber says "Sorry — I didn't quite catch that" Nothing is broken — just say your message again.
The page won't load Check your internet, then refresh. Your session is saved.
I closed the app mid-session Open My Sessions → your session is there marked In progress.
I can't sign in Ask your teacher or organisation to check your account.
I sent a message by mistake It's fine — just keep going, or leave the session.

12. Stage 5 — your own assessment

When you finish a level, your teacher starts a review. Part of it is your voice: a short self-assessment where you say how you feel about your progress. You'll see a card on your dashboard when it's your turn — press it and answer honestly. Your teacher and organisation confirm the level together with you.

Words like "placement", "level", and "scenario" are explained in the glossary.
